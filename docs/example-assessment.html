        How it is computed

        From controls to techniques

        Each control implements MITRE mitigations, from ATT&CK (M1032) or ATLAS (AML.M0020), or counters techniques directly. A mitigation is as strong as the most mature control implementing it, and a technique as hard as the strongest mitigation MITRE lists against it (its sub-techniques' included).

        With a maturity m on a scale of 5: m/5 below 40% leaves a technique facilitated, below 80% partly hindered, and from 80% hindered. A technique MITRE lists no preventive mitigation for, such as most of Reconnaissance, has no preventive control.

        The easiest way through

        At each stage, the path takes a facilitated technique first, then one no control prevents, then the least hindered, and among equals the one seen in the logs, then the most common in real attacks: used by the most known groups, campaigns and malware for ATT&CK, the most realized for ATLAS. It is blocked at the first stage where even the easiest technique is hindered.

        The exposure index (0 to 100) is the mean of 1 − m/5 over the assessed techniques controls can prevent, weighted by how common each one is.

        What it does not say

        "Facilitated" says that a control gap leaves a known technique without its main counter-measure, as MITRE maps them. It is not a probability of attack, and a real attack does not go through every stage. Read it with the people who know the systems.
